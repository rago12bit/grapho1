    03 · Planted faults

    Hundreds of faults planted on Kestrel's topology, each with decoys.

    Five kinds: a deep change with alerts climbing to it, a silent one whose cause never alerts, an infrastructure failure with nothing changed, one change breaking two services, and a failing host. Each scenario carries a closer change, a newer one and an old one on the path. The generator is ours, so this measures the patterns the judge was built for; the held-out seeds are the fairer number.

    Switch one factor off · top-1, seed 7, 150 scenarios

    When it says…

    Confidence comes from the margin over the runner-up, not the raw score. It is conservative and ordered: high is right more often than medium, medium more than low.

    04 · Learning

    Each company's judge, refitted on its own solved incidents.

    A logistic model over the judge's factors plus each service's track record, used only if, cross-validated on solved incidents alone, it names the cause at least as often as the hand-set weights. The open incidents are the test.

    07 · Limits

    What these numbers don't say.

    • It is not the most accurate ranker on held-out Train Ticket: NSigma is, on both AC@1 and Avg@5.
    • 270 of the 360 RCAEval cases were used to develop v2 and v3; read those as seen. The 90 held-out cases are the honest test.
    • Trace methods (MicroRank, TraceRCA) were not run here; their numbers are quoted as published.
    • The planted-fault generator is ours. Silent causes are the weak spot (72–84% on held-out seeds).
    • Like every baseline, the detector uses the injection time to split normal from faulty data.
    • The scale company is synthetic; the timings are a laptop's, not a cluster's.
